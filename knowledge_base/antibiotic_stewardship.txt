# Antibiotic Stewardship Principles

## Core Principles
Antibiotic stewardship programs (ASPs) aim to optimize antimicrobial use to improve patient outcomes, reduce antimicrobial resistance, and decrease healthcare costs.

## The Five Rights of Antibiotic Prescribing
1. **Right drug**: Based on likely pathogen and local resistance patterns
2. **Right dose**: Pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic optimization
3. **Right route**: IV-to-oral transition when appropriate
4. **Right duration**: Shortest effective duration
5. **Right time**: Prompt initiation for serious infections; avoid unnecessary empiric therapy

## Common Conditions and Recommended Durations

### Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):
- Minimum 5 days (if clinically stable for ≥48 hours)
- Typical regimen: Amoxicillin 1g TID or Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg BID
- Atypical coverage: Add azithromycin 500 mg day 1, then 250 mg days 2–5
- Severe/ICU: Ceftriaxone 1g IV daily + azithromycin 500 mg IV daily

### Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):
- Uncomplicated cystitis: Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID x 5 days (first-line) or TMP/SMX DS BID x 3 days
- Complicated UTI/Pyelonephritis: Fluoroquinolone x 5–7 days or ceftriaxone 1g IV daily
- Avoid fluoroquinolones for uncomplicated UTI (reserve for serious infections)

### Skin and Soft Tissue Infections (SSTI):
- Cellulitis (non-purulent): Cephalexin 500 mg QID x 5–7 days
- Purulent SSTI/abscess: I&D + TMP/SMX DS BID or doxycycline 100 mg BID x 5–7 days
- Severe: Vancomycin IV + piperacillin-tazobactam IV

### Intra-Abdominal Infections:
- Mild-moderate: Ceftriaxone + metronidazole or amoxicillin-clavulanate
- Severe: Piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem
- Duration: 4 days post source control (STOP-IT trial)

## Key Stewardship Interventions
1. **Prospective audit and feedback**: Infectious disease review of antibiotic orders
2. **Prior authorization**: Restrict broad-spectrum agents (carbapenems, vancomycin)
3. **Antibiogram**: Facility-specific resistance data to guide empiric therapy
4. **De-escalation**: Narrow spectrum based on culture results within 48–72 hours
5. **IV-to-oral conversion**: Switch when clinically improving and tolerating oral intake
6. **Procalcitonin-guided therapy**: Helps differentiate bacterial vs. viral infection; guides discontinuation

## Antimicrobial Resistance Concerns
### ESKAPE Pathogens:
- Enterococcus faecium (VRE)
- Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)
- Klebsiella pneumoniae (ESBL, CRE)
- Acinetobacter baumannii (MDR)
- Pseudomonas aeruginosa (MDR)
- Enterobacter species (AmpC)

### Strategies to Combat Resistance:
- Avoid unnecessary antibiotic prescribing (esp. for viral infections)
- Use narrow-spectrum agents when possible
- Complete full courses (but avoid unnecessarily long courses)
- Implement infection prevention measures (hand hygiene, isolation)
- Surveillance cultures for MDR organisms in high-risk patients
