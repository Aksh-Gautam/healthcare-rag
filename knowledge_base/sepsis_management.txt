# Sepsis and Septic Shock Management

## Definitions
- **Sepsis**: Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. Defined as suspected infection + acute increase in SOFA score ≥2.
- **Septic Shock**: Sepsis with persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg AND serum lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

## Quick SOFA (qSOFA) Screening
Bedside screening for sepsis (≥2 criteria suggests higher mortality risk):
- Respiratory rate ≥22/min
- Altered mentation (GCS <15)
- Systolic blood pressure ≤100 mmHg

## Hour-1 Bundle (Initiate within 1 hour)
1. **Measure lactate level**: Remeasure within 2–4 hours if initial lactate >2 mmol/L
2. **Obtain blood cultures**: Before administering antibiotics (at least 2 sets: 1 aerobic + 1 anaerobic from 2 different sites)
3. **Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics**: 
   - Community-acquired: Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV OR meropenem 1g IV
   - Hospital-acquired/MRSA risk: Add vancomycin 25–30 mg/kg IV loading dose
   - Suspected atypical: Add azithromycin 500 mg IV
4. **Begin rapid fluid resuscitation**: 30 mL/kg crystalloid (Lactated Ringer's preferred over normal saline) for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L
5. **Apply vasopressors**: If hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation, target MAP ≥65 mmHg

## Vasopressor Therapy
First-line: **Norepinephrine** 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min (preferred)
Second-line: Add **vasopressin** 0.03 units/min (when norepinephrine dose is 0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min)
Third-line: Add **epinephrine** 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min
- Central venous access preferred but peripheral acceptable initially
- Arterial line for continuous BP monitoring

## Corticosteroids
- **Hydrocortisone 200 mg/day IV** (50 mg IV q6h or continuous infusion)
- Indicated when adequate fluid resuscitation and vasopressor therapy fail to restore hemodynamic stability
- Duration: Until vasopressors are discontinued, then taper

## Source Control
- Identify and control the source of infection within 6–12 hours
- Drain abscesses, debride infected necrotic tissue, remove infected devices
- Choose the least invasive effective intervention

## Fluid Management After Resuscitation
- Use dynamic measures to guide fluid responsiveness (pulse pressure variation, passive leg raise)
- Avoid fluid overload; transition to conservative fluid strategy once hemodynamically stable
- Target neutral fluid balance by day 3

## Antibiotic Management
- Reassess antimicrobial therapy daily for de-escalation
- Procalcitonin-guided de-escalation if available
- Typical duration: 7–10 days (shorter courses may be appropriate)
- Longer courses for: endocarditis, osteomyelitis, undrainable abscesses

## Monitoring
- Lactate clearance (target ≥10% decrease per 2 hours)
- Urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr
- Mean arterial pressure ≥65 mmHg
- Central venous oxygen saturation (ScvO2) ≥70%
- Serial organ function assessment (SOFA score)
