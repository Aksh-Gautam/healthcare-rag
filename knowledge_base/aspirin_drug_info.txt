# Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid) in Cardiovascular Disease

## Mechanism of Action
Aspirin irreversibly inhibits cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), blocking thromboxane A2 synthesis in platelets. This results in:
- Inhibition of platelet aggregation for the lifetime of the platelet (7–10 days)
- Antithrombotic effect at low doses (75–325 mg)
- Anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects at higher doses (>1g)

## Cardiovascular Indications

### Secondary Prevention (Strong recommendation):
- Acute coronary syndrome (STEMI, NSTEMI, unstable angina)
- Post-PCI/stent placement (part of DAPT)
- Stable ischemic heart disease
- History of ischemic stroke or TIA (non-cardioembolic)
- Peripheral arterial disease

### Primary Prevention (Weak/conditional):
- NOT routinely recommended for primary prevention (2019 ACC/AHA guideline change)
- May be considered in select patients aged 40–70 with elevated ASCVD risk (≥10% 10-year) AND low bleeding risk
- NOT recommended for adults >70 years for primary prevention
- NOT recommended if increased bleeding risk

## Dosing
- Acute MI/ACS: 162–325 mg chewed (non-enteric coated) immediately, then 81 mg daily
- Secondary prevention: 75–100 mg once daily (81 mg in US)
- Post-PCI with stent: 81 mg daily indefinitely (part of DAPT)
- Ischemic stroke/TIA: 50–325 mg daily (commonly 81 mg)

## Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT)
Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor after ACS or PCI:
- Minimum duration: 6–12 months after drug-eluting stent
- Extended DAPT (>12 months): Consider if high ischemic risk and low bleeding risk
- Shortened DAPT (1–3 months): Consider if high bleeding risk, followed by P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy

## Contraindications
- Active peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding
- Known aspirin allergy or NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease
- Bleeding disorders (hemophilia, severe thrombocytopenia)
- Children/adolescents with viral illness (Reye syndrome risk)

## Adverse Effects
- GI bleeding: Most common serious adverse effect (dose-related)
- Intracranial hemorrhage: Small increased risk
- GI upset, dyspepsia, nausea
- Tinnitus (at higher doses)
- Bruising
- Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (aspirin-sensitive asthma)

## Drug Interactions
- Warfarin/DOACs: Significantly increased bleeding risk
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen): May antagonize antiplatelet effect of aspirin (take aspirin 30 min before ibuprofen)
- SSRIs/SNRIs: Increased bleeding risk
- Methotrexate: Reduced clearance at high aspirin doses
- ACE inhibitors: May reduce antihypertensive effect

## Aspirin Resistance
- Prevalence: 5–45% depending on definition and assay
- Consider platelet function testing if recurrent events on aspirin
- Management: Ensure compliance, adequate dosing; switch to alternative antiplatelet
