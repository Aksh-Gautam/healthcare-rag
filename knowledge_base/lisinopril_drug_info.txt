# Lisinopril

## Classification
- Class: Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitor
- Brand names: Prinivil, Zestril

## Mechanism of Action
Lisinopril competitively inhibits angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), preventing conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II. This results in:
1. Decreased aldosterone secretion → reduced sodium and water retention
2. Vasodilation of arterioles and venules → reduced blood pressure
3. Decreased cardiac preload and afterload
4. Reduced cardiac remodeling after myocardial infarction
5. Renoprotective effects via reduced intraglomerular pressure

## Indications
- Hypertension (first-line therapy)
- Heart failure (HFrEF) — reduces mortality and hospitalizations
- Acute myocardial infarction (within 24 hours to improve survival)
- Diabetic nephropathy — slows progression of kidney disease

## Dosing
### Hypertension:
- Starting: 10 mg once daily (5 mg if on diuretic or elderly)
- Titration: Increase by 10 mg at 2-week intervals
- Usual maintenance: 20–40 mg once daily
- Maximum: 80 mg/day

### Heart Failure:
- Starting: 2.5–5 mg once daily
- Target: 20–40 mg once daily
- Titrate slowly (every 2 weeks)

### Post-MI:
- Starting: 5 mg within 24 hours, then 5 mg at 24 hours, 10 mg at 48 hours
- Maintenance: 10 mg once daily

## Contraindications
- History of angioedema related to ACE inhibitor use
- Hereditary or idiopathic angioedema
- Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes
- Bilateral renal artery stenosis
- Pregnancy (teratogenic — Category D)

## Adverse Effects
### Common:
- Dry cough (5–20%): Due to bradykinin accumulation; most common reason for discontinuation
- Dizziness, headache
- Hyperkalemia (especially with renal impairment or potassium-sparing agents)
- Hypotension (especially first dose or volume-depleted patients)

### Serious:
- Angioedema (0.1–0.7%): Swelling of face, lips, tongue, throat — can be life-threatening
  - Higher risk in Black patients
  - Requires immediate discontinuation and emergency treatment
- Acute kidney injury (especially if bilateral renal artery stenosis)
- Fetal toxicity (second and third trimester)

## Drug Interactions
- Potassium supplements/potassium-sparing diuretics: Increased hyperkalemia risk
- NSAIDs: Reduced antihypertensive effect; increased nephrotoxicity
- Lithium: Increased lithium levels (reduced renal clearance)
- Sacubitril: DO NOT combine (36-hour washout required before switching to sacubitril/valsartan)
- Aliskiren: Contraindicated combination in diabetes

## Monitoring
- Blood pressure: 2–4 weeks after initiation or dose change
- Serum creatinine and potassium: At baseline, 1–2 weeks after initiation, then periodically
- Acceptable creatinine rise: Up to 30% from baseline
- Discontinue if creatinine rises >50% or potassium >5.5 mEq/L
