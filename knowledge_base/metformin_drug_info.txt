# Metformin Hydrochloride

## Classification
- Class: Biguanide antihyperglycemic agent
- Brand names: Glucophage, Glucophage XR, Fortamet, Glumetza, Riomet

## Mechanism of Action
Metformin works through multiple mechanisms:
1. Decreases hepatic glucose production (primary mechanism) by inhibiting gluconeogenesis
2. Increases insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues (muscle, fat)
3. Decreases intestinal absorption of glucose
4. Does NOT stimulate insulin secretion (does not cause hypoglycemia when used alone)
5. Activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway

## Indications
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (first-line therapy per ADA guidelines)
- Prediabetes/diabetes prevention (off-label but evidence-based)
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) — off-label
- Gestational diabetes (limited evidence)

## Dosing
### Immediate Release:
- Starting: 500 mg orally twice daily with meals, OR 850 mg once daily with meals
- Titration: Increase by 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every 2 weeks
- Maximum: 2,550 mg/day in divided doses (850 mg TID)

### Extended Release:
- Starting: 500 mg once daily with evening meal
- Titration: Increase by 500 mg weekly
- Maximum: 2,000 mg once daily

## Contraindications
- eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² (absolute)
- eGFR 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m² (do not initiate; may continue with monitoring if already on)
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin

## Adverse Effects
### Common (>10%):
- Gastrointestinal: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, flatulence, abdominal discomfort (up to 30%)
- Metallic taste
- These effects are dose-related and often resolve with time

### Serious:
- Lactic acidosis (rare but potentially fatal; risk 3–10 per 100,000 patient-years)
  - Risk factors: renal impairment, hepatic disease, excessive alcohol, dehydration, sepsis
- Vitamin B12 deficiency (10–30% of long-term users; monitor levels)

## Drug Interactions
- Alcohol: Increases risk of lactic acidosis
- Iodinated contrast media: Hold metformin for 48 hours after contrast; restart after verifying renal function
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (topiramate, zonisamide): Increased risk of lactic acidosis
- Cimetidine: Increases metformin levels (competes for renal tubular secretion)

## Special Populations
- Elderly: Start at lowest dose; monitor renal function regularly
- Pregnancy: Category B; generally discontinued (insulin preferred)
- Hepatic impairment: Avoid (increased risk of lactic acidosis)

## Monitoring
- HbA1c every 3–6 months
- Renal function (eGFR) at least annually
- Vitamin B12 levels annually with long-term use
- CBC if symptoms of anemia develop

## Benefits Beyond Glucose Control
- Weight neutral to slight weight loss (1–3 kg)
- No hypoglycemia risk as monotherapy
- Possible cardiovascular benefit (UKPDS study)
- Low cost; widely available as generic
