# Statin Therapy for Cardiovascular Prevention

## Overview
Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) are the cornerstone of lipid-lowering therapy. This summary covers major trial evidence supporting their use in primary and secondary cardiovascular prevention.

## Key Clinical Evidence

### CTT Meta-Analysis (Cholesterol Treatment Trialists)
- Included 26 randomized trials with >170,000 patients
- Each 1 mmol/L (39 mg/dL) reduction in LDL-C reduces:
  - Major vascular events by 22%
  - Major coronary events by 24%
  - Stroke by 15%
  - All-cause mortality by 10%
- Benefit consistent across all subgroups (age, sex, diabetes status, baseline LDL)
- Absolute benefit proportional to baseline cardiovascular risk

### JUPITER Trial (Rosuvastatin 20mg)
- 17,802 healthy individuals with LDL <130 mg/dL and hsCRP ≥2 mg/L
- 44% reduction in major cardiovascular events
- Demonstrated benefit even in those with "normal" LDL levels
- NNT: 25 over 5 years for major cardiovascular event prevention

### 4S Trial (Simvastatin 20-40mg)
- 4,444 patients with prior MI or angina; LDL 188 mg/dL
- 30% reduction in all-cause mortality
- 42% reduction in coronary death
- Landmark trial establishing mortality benefit

### PROVE-IT TIMI 22 (Intensive vs. Moderate Statin)
- Atorvastatin 80 mg vs. pravastatin 40 mg post-ACS
- 16% further reduction in events with high-intensity statin
- Established "lower is better" principle for LDL-C

## Statin Intensity Classification

### High-Intensity (lowers LDL ≥50%):
- Atorvastatin 40–80 mg
- Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg

### Moderate-Intensity (lowers LDL 30–49%):
- Atorvastatin 10–20 mg
- Rosuvastatin 5–10 mg
- Simvastatin 20–40 mg
- Pravastatin 40–80 mg

### Low-Intensity (lowers LDL <30%):
- Simvastatin 10 mg
- Pravastatin 10–20 mg

## Indications per ACC/AHA Guidelines
1. Clinical ASCVD (secondary prevention): High-intensity statin
2. LDL ≥190 mg/dL: High-intensity statin (regardless of risk score)
3. Diabetes, age 40–75: Moderate-intensity statin (high-intensity if 10-year risk ≥7.5%)
4. 10-year ASCVD risk ≥7.5%, age 40–75: Moderate-to-high intensity statin

## Adverse Effects
- Myalgia (5–10%): Most common reason for discontinuation; true myopathy rare
- Rhabdomyolysis: Very rare (<0.1%); risk increased with high doses, drug interactions
- Hepatotoxicity: Transaminase elevation; routine monitoring no longer recommended
- New-onset diabetes: Small increase (0.1–0.3% per year); benefits outweigh risk
- Cognitive effects: No convincing evidence from trials; FDA label warning based on case reports

## Non-Statin Therapies (Add-on if LDL not at goal)
- Ezetimibe: Reduces LDL by additional 18–25%
- PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab, alirocumab): Reduce LDL by 50–60%; proven cardiovascular outcomes benefit
- Bempedoic acid: Reduces LDL by 15–25%; no myalgia risk
- Inclisiran: siRNA therapy; reduces LDL by 50%; twice-yearly injection
