# Warfarin (Coumadin)

## Mechanism of Action
Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1), blocking the recycling of vitamin K. This prevents the gamma-carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors:
- Factor II (prothrombin)
- Factor VII
- Factor IX
- Factor X
- Also inhibits protein C and protein S (natural anticoagulants)

Onset of anticoagulant effect: 2–7 days (depends on half-lives of existing clotting factors). Factor VII has shortest half-life (~6 hours), but full effect requires depletion of Factor II (~60 hours).

## Indications
- Atrial fibrillation (stroke prevention) — though DOACs now preferred
- Mechanical heart valves (ONLY oral anticoagulant approved)
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) treatment/prevention
- Left ventricular thrombus
- Antiphospholipid syndrome

## Dosing and INR Targets
### Initiation:
- Starting dose: 5 mg daily (lower in elderly, malnourished, liver disease, genetic variants)
- Pharmacogenomic dosing: Consider CYP2C9 and VKORC1 genotyping if available
- Overlap with parenteral anticoagulant (heparin/LMWH) for minimum 5 days AND until INR ≥2.0 for ≥24 hours

### INR Targets:
- Most indications (AF, DVT/PE): INR 2.0–3.0
- Mechanical mitral valve: INR 2.5–3.5
- Mechanical aortic valve (newer generation): INR 2.0–3.0

## Drug and Food Interactions (CRITICAL)
### Drugs that INCREASE warfarin effect (↑ INR):
- Amiodarone, fluconazole, metronidazole, TMP/SMX
- Ciprofloxacin, fluoxetine, omeprazole
- Acetaminophen (high doses >2g/day)

### Drugs that DECREASE warfarin effect (↓ INR):
- Rifampin (most potent inducer), carbamazepine, phenytoin
- St. John's Wort, ginseng
- Cholestyramine, sucralfate

### Food Interactions:
- Vitamin K-rich foods: Maintain CONSISTENT intake (not avoidance)
  - High vitamin K: Kale, spinach, collard greens, broccoli, Brussels sprouts
- Cranberry juice: May increase INR
- Alcohol: Variable effect; binge drinking increases INR

## Monitoring
- INR monitoring: Every 2–3 days during initiation, then weekly, then every 4 weeks when stable
- Time in therapeutic range (TTR): Goal ≥70%
- More frequent monitoring with: illness, diet changes, new medications, alcohol changes

## Reversal
### Vitamin K (Phytonadione):
- INR 4.5–10 (no bleeding): Hold warfarin; consider vitamin K 1–2.5 mg orally
- INR >10 (no bleeding): Vitamin K 2.5–5 mg orally
- Serious/life-threatening bleeding: IV vitamin K 10 mg slow infusion + 4-factor PCC (preferred) or FFP

### 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC):
- Rapid reversal for major bleeding or urgent surgery
- Contains factors II, VII, IX, X
- Dose based on INR and weight

## Adverse Effects
- Bleeding (major bleeding rate: 1–3% per year)
- Skin necrosis (rare; usually days 3–8; associated with protein C deficiency)
- Purple toe syndrome (rare; cholesterol microembolization)
- Teratogenic (pregnancy category X) — avoid in pregnancy
